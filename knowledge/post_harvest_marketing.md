# Post-harvest & marketing — turning yield into income

India loses 10–30% of produce after harvest. Fixing post-harvest is often worth more than growing more.

## Right moisture for storage (learn these 6 numbers)
- Cereals (wheat/paddy/maize): 10–12% | Pulses: 9–10% | Oilseeds (soybean/mustard): 8–10% | Onion (cured): dry & ventilated | Potato (cured): 10–12°C cold store | Tomato: 10–12°C, never fridge-cold
- Rule: if a grain snaps (doesn't bend) or a soybean pod rattles — near storage moisture. Damp grain = fungus, weevils, price cut, and aflatoxin risk (health hazard).

## Grading — the free price bump
- Clean (remove straw, stones, dirt, broken & diseased grains), sort by size/colour. Same lot, graded vs ungraded: commonly 8–15% better price and faster sale.
- Simple wire-mesh sieves (2 sizes) + a tarpaulin = your grading unit. For onion/potato/tomato: sort by size class, remove cut/bruised/diseased — sell Grade A separately, never mixed.

## Packing that protects price
- Grains: new/stitched gunny sacks on pallets, off the floor, away from walls (moisture).
- Vegetables: ventilated plastic crates (stack max 3–4), never jute sacks for tomato (bruising = 20% loss).
- Onion: net/jute bags, ventilated storage. Banana: lined crates, no heavy stacking.

## Storage structures
- Hermetic/PICS bags (grain & pulses): airtight, no chemical, kills weevils — pulses stay clean 6–12 months.
- Metal bins (household-scale), covered 'kunbi' type bamboo onion chawl (ventilated, dry), community cold stores for potato.
- Golden rule: only store WELL-DRIED, graded produce; monitor monthly for moisture/pests.

## When to sell (the real skill)
- At harvest: prices are lowest (glut). If you must sell for cash needs, sell the minimum; store the rest if storage is possible.
- Stagger: onion Apr–Dec, potato via cold store till Nov, pulses/oilseeds 2–6 months — lean-season prices are often 30–100% higher (verify trend for your crop on eNAM/AgriEasy history).
- MSP crops (wheat, paddy, etc.): MSP + bonus procurement is your risk-free floor at procurement centres; open market when it beats MSP.
- Watch: monsoon forecasts, festival demand, import/export news — they move prices; ask the assistant "what should I watch before selling X".

## Marketing channels (use more than one)
1. Local mandi/APMC — fast, but fees + transporter margins; keep weight slip always.
2. eNAM (online national mandi) — register with bank + mobile; wider buyer pool for supported mandis.
3. AgriEasy (agrieasy.site) — list graded lots with photos; sell direct to bulk buyers/processors/retailers; keep payments on-platform (see selling_on_agrieasy.md).
4. FPO — farmer-producer organization: aggregates members' produce, negotiates better prices, shares grading/cold-store infrastructure. If there's an FPO within reach, joining usually pays.
5. Direct-to-consumer/local retail — highest margin for vegetables, milk, pulses (needs consistent grading & delivery).

## Value addition (more ₹ per quintal)
- Pulses → dal (custom milling), oilseeds → oil + sell cake as cattle feed, sugarcane → jaggery/organic jaggery, tomato → pulp, banana → chips, milk → ghee. Even simple cleaning+grading+branding with your FPO is "value addition".

## Records & digital payment (do this from day 1)
- Per-lot record: crop, grade, weight, buyer, rate, date, payment mode. UPI/bank transfer recorded in the app — cash only with a signed receipt.
- Digital payment = proof for crop loans (KCC), insurance claims (PMFBY), and repeat-buyer trust on AgriEasy.

## Golden sequence before every sale
1. Harvest at right maturity 2. Dry/cure properly 3. Clean & grade 4. Weigh accurately (own tally) 5. Check live prices (AgriEasy/eNAM/mandi) 6. List/negotiate 7. Written or in-app order 8. Payment on platform/UPI before or at delivery as agreed 9. Save record & rate the buyer.
